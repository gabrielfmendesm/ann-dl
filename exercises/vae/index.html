    VAE — Variational Autoencoders

    A variational autoencoder trained on MNIST twice: first with a 16-dimensional latent space, logging the two terms of its loss separately, then with a 2-dimensional one that can be drawn, to see what the prior buys and what it costs. The model is built and trained with PyTorch and autograd, as the statement allows; every VAE-specific part is written out in code/vae.py: the reparameterization, the reconstruction term (the binary cross-entropy summed over the 784 pixels), the closed-form KL to the prior, and the sampling. torchvision only loads MNIST, and SciPy only supplies Φ⁻¹ (scipy.stats.norm.ppf). Both loss terms are reported in nats per image: summed over the pixels (or the latent dimensions), averaged over the images.

    Approach. The code is split into files under code/: vae.py (the model, the reparameterization, both loss terms, the sampling and the training loop), common.py (settings, the MNIST split and plotting helpers), exercise1.py and exercise2.py (the experiments, the figures and the measurements behind each analysis), loss_check.py (checks of the hand-written terms against independent computations) and main.py, which runs everything and writes results/results.json; check_report.py then confirms that the numbers of this report match that file. Every number and figure in this report is reproduced by installing requirements.txt and running, from the repository root:

    python docs/exercises/vae/code/main.py
python docs/exercises/vae/code/check_report.py

    The first run downloads MNIST into data/MNIST (not committed). The whole script takes about 80 seconds on a laptop CPU; the results below were produced with torch 2.15.0 and torchvision 0.29.2, and two runs give identical numbers.

    Seeds. torch.manual_seed(42) is called right before building each of the two models, so both start from the same seed; the mini-batch shuffling and the ε of training come from that same generator. The split uses torch.Generator().manual_seed(42), exactly as given, and the prior samples of Figure 3 are drawn after torch.manual_seed(0). Evaluation draws its ε from generators of its own (seed 1 for the validation set, seed 2 for the test set), so measuring a model never changes its training, and every epoch is evaluated on the validation set with the same ε.

    Training. Both models use the recipe of the statement: Adam with learning rate 10⁻³, batches of 128, 20 epochs, β = 1. After each epoch both terms are logged on the training set (the average over the epoch's batches) and on the validation set (measured at the end of the epoch). No decision is taken during training: the budget is fixed by the statement, with no early stopping and no model selection, so the validation set is only monitored. The test set is used once training is over, for the final numbers and for the figures the statement draws from it.

    Challenges.

    • Hand-written terms that can be trusted. loss_check.py compares each term with an independent computation. The reconstruction term equals F.binary_cross_entropy(..., reduction="sum") divided by the batch size, the call the statement names, to a relative 9.2 × 10⁻⁸ on all 10 000 validation images; its gradient agrees to 7.7 × 10⁻⁸ wherever the built-in function does not floor x̂(1 − x̂) at 10⁻¹² in its backward pass, which happens on 3.49% of the pixels, all but 1 of them background pixels. The closed-form KL matches a Monte Carlo estimate with 200 000 samples to within 2.04 standard errors (0.06%), and the reparameterization passes the gradients ∂z/∂μ = 1 and ∂z/∂log σ² = σε/2 exactly.
    • The reduction of the reconstruction term. Summing over the pixels and averaging over the images keeps the two terms on the same scale. reduction="mean" would divide the reconstruction term by 784.00 (checked in loss_check.py), the same as training with β ≈ 784.
    • An answer to every analysis question, with a number. Each claim in items 1E and 2E rests on a measurement printed by the code: the density of the cloud of training codes at the samples of Figure 3, the share of grey pixels, the information a KL can carry, the spread of the reconstructions, the ink of each interpolated image.

    Exercise 1

    A VAE on MNIST.

    A — Load and split the data

    MNIST is loaded with torchvision.datasets.MNIST(..., download=True, transform=transforms.ToTensor()), which puts the pixels in [0, 1] (the range of the training images is [0.0, 1.0]), and each 28 × 28 image is flattened into a 784-vector. The 60 000 training images are split with random_split(train_full, [50_000, 10_000], generator=torch.Generator().manual_seed(42)), exactly as in the statement: 50 000 images for training and 10 000 for validation. The 10 000 official test images are the test set. The only change to the statement's lines is the root folder: data/ at the repository root, written as an absolute path so the script runs from any directory (load_mnist in code/common.py).

    B — Build the model

    Part Layers
    Encoder 784 → 512 → 256, ReLU after each
    Heads two linear layers 256 → d: one for μ, one for log σ²
    Decoder d → 256 → 512 → 784, ReLU after the hidden layers, sigmoid at the output

    With d = 16 the model has 1 079 600 parameters. The loss of one image, minimized on the average over the batch, is the negative ELBO:

    \[ L = \underbrace{-\sum_{j=1}^{784} \Big[x_j \log \hat{x}_j + (1 - x_j) \log (1 - \hat{x}_j)\Big]}_{\text{reconstruction (BCE)}} \;+\; \beta \cdot \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d} \left(\mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2 - 1 - \log \sigma_i^2\right)}_{\text{KL}}, \qquad \beta = 1 . \]

    The four VAE-specific parts are the functions of code/vae.py, all written out:

    • reparameterize: z = μ + σ ⊙ ε with σ = exp(½ log σ²) and ε ~ N(0, I). The noise enters as an input, so the gradient of the reconstruction reaches both heads.
    • reconstruction_term: −log p(x | z) for a decoder that outputs, per pixel, the probability \(\hat{x}_j\) that it is "on" (a Bernoulli likelihood). It is the formula above, summed over the 784 pixels and averaged over the images. As in F.binary_cross_entropy, each log is floored at −100, so a saturated sigmoid would cost 100 nats rather than an infinite loss.
    • kl_term: the KL from N(μ, σ²) to N(0, I) in closed form, summed over the d dimensions and averaged over the images; torch.distributions is not used anywhere.
    • sample: decodes z ~ N(0, I).
    code/vae.py
    """The VAE of the statement: the model, the reparameterization, both loss terms, the sampling and the training loop.

Autograd computes the gradients (the statement allows it), but every VAE-specific part is written out here:
the reparameterization z = mu + sigma * eps, the reconstruction term (the binary cross-entropy summed over the
784 pixels), the closed-form KL to the prior N(0, I), and the sampling from that prior. Both loss terms are
returned per image, in nats: summed over pixels (or latent dimensions), averaged over the images of the batch.
"""

import torch
from torch import nn
from torch.utils.data import DataLoader, TensorDataset

N_PIXELS = 28 * 28
LOG_FLOOR = -100.0  # the floor F.binary_cross_entropy puts on each log; loss_check.py compares the two


class VAE(nn.Module):
    """Encoder 784 -> 512 -> 256 (ReLU after each), heads 256 -> d for mu and log sigma^2,
    decoder d -> 256 -> 512 -> 784 (ReLU after the hidden layers, sigmoid at the output)."""

    def __init__(self, d):
        super().__init__()
        self.d = d
        self.encoder = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(N_PIXELS, 512), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(512, 256), nn.ReLU())
        self.mu_head = nn.Linear(256, d)
        self.logvar_head = nn.Linear(256, d)  # log sigma^2: unconstrained, so sigma^2 = exp(.) is always positive
        self.decoder = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(d, 256), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(256, 512), nn.ReLU(),
                                     nn.Linear(512, N_PIXELS), nn.Sigmoid())

    def encode(self, x):
        """q(z|x) = N(mu, diag(sigma^2)): returns mu and log sigma^2, each of shape (batch, d)."""
        h = self.encoder(x)
        return self.mu_head(h), self.logvar_head(h)

    def decode(self, z):
        """p(x|z): the probability that each of the 784 pixels is "on" (a Bernoulli mean, in (0, 1))."""
        return self.decoder(z)

    def forward(self, x, generator=None):
        mu, logvar = self.encode(x)
        z = reparameterize(mu, logvar, generator)
        return self.decode(z), mu, logvar


def reparameterize(mu, logvar, generator=None):
    """z = mu + sigma * eps with eps ~ N(0, I): the randomness enters as an input, so gradients reach
    mu (dz/dmu = 1) and log sigma^2 (dz/dlogvar = sigma * eps / 2). generator=None draws eps from the
    global RNG (training); evaluation passes its own generator so that it does not move the global one."""
    sigma = torch.exp(0.5 * logvar)
    eps = torch.randn(mu.shape, generator=generator)
    return mu + sigma * eps


def reconstruction_term(x_hat, x):
    """-log p(x|z) for a Bernoulli decoder: -sum_j [x_j log x_hat_j + (1 - x_j) log(1 - x_hat_j)], the binary
    cross-entropy summed over the 784 pixels and averaged over the images. Each log is floored at -100,
    exactly as F.binary_cross_entropy does, so a saturated sigmoid output would cost 100 nats, not infinity."""
    log_p = torch.log(x_hat).clamp(min=LOG_FLOOR)       # log x_hat
    log_1mp = torch.log1p(-x_hat).clamp(min=LOG_FLOOR)  # log(1 - x_hat)
    per_image = -(x * log_p + (1 - x) * log_1mp).sum(dim=1)
    return per_image.mean()


def kl_term(mu, logvar):
    """KL(N(mu, sigma^2) || N(0, I)) in closed form, 1/2 sum_i (mu_i^2 + sigma_i^2 - 1 - log sigma_i^2),
    summed over the d latent dimensions and averaged over the images."""
    per_image = 0.5 * (mu ** 2 + logvar.exp() - 1 - logvar).sum(dim=1)
    return per_image.mean()


def sample(model, n):
    """n images decoded from z ~ N(0, I), drawn from the global RNG (the caller sets torch.manual_seed)."""
    z = torch.randn(n, model.d)
    return model.decode(z)


@torch.no_grad()
def evaluate(model, X, seed, chunk=1000):
    """Both terms on a whole set, in nats per image. z is sampled as in training (one eps per image), from a
    generator of its own, so the estimate is repeatable and does not touch the training RNG."""
    model.eval()
    generator = torch.Generator().manual_seed(seed)
    rec = kl = 0.0
    for start in range(0, len(X), chunk):
        x = X[start:start + chunk]
        x_hat, mu, logvar = model(x, generator)
        rec += reconstruction_term(x_hat, x).item() * len(x)
        kl += kl_term(mu, logvar).item() * len(x)
    return rec / len(X), kl / len(X)


def train(model, X_train, X_val, epochs, batch_size, lr, beta, val_seed):
    """Adam on the negative ELBO, L = reconstruction + beta * KL, one update per mini-batch.

    Logs both terms separately: on the training set, the average over the epoch's batches (weighted by
    batch size); on the validation set, measured at the end of the epoch with the same eps every epoch
    (generator seeded with val_seed). Entry 0 of the validation lists is the untrained model."""
    optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=lr)
    loader = DataLoader(TensorDataset(X_train), batch_size=batch_size, shuffle=True)  # shuffles with the global RNG
    val_rec, val_kl = evaluate(model, X_val, val_seed)
    history = {"train_rec": [], "train_kl": [], "val_rec": [val_rec], "val_kl": [val_kl]}
    for _ in range(epochs):
        model.train()
        rec_sum = kl_sum = 0.0
        for (x,) in loader:
            x_hat, mu, logvar = model(x)
            rec, kl = reconstruction_term(x_hat, x), kl_term(mu, logvar)
            loss = rec + beta * kl
            if not torch.isfinite(loss):
                raise FloatingPointError("the loss is not finite")
            optimizer.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            optimizer.step()
            rec_sum += rec.item() * len(x)
            kl_sum += kl.item() * len(x)
        history["train_rec"].append(rec_sum / len(X_train))
        history["train_kl"].append(kl_sum / len(X_train))
        val_rec, val_kl = evaluate(model, X_val, val_seed)
        history["val_rec"].append(val_rec)
        history["val_kl"].append(val_kl)
    return history

    E — Analysis

    Why the optimizer pays more KL. The KL is the price of a code that says something about the image. Averaged over the data it splits into the information the code carries about x and the mismatch between the cloud of all codes and the prior (the latent-space lecture's split, with q(z) the aggregate posterior):

    \[ \mathrm{E}_x\big[\mathrm{KL}(q(z \mid x)\,\|\,p(z))\big] = I_q(x; z) + \mathrm{KL}(q(z)\,\|\,p(z)) . \]

    The untrained encoder returns almost the prior for every image (KL 0.0291 nats), a code that tells the decoder almost nothing about which image it came from. With no information in the code the decoder can only output one image for every input, and the best such image for the test set, the mean test image, still costs 205.65 nats of reconstruction. The optimizer minimizes the sum of the two terms, so it buys information while a nat of KL saves more than a nat of reconstruction. Between epoch 1 and epoch 20 the validation KL rose by 6.07 nats (13.37 to 19.44) while the reconstruction fell by 47.03 (129.82 to 82.79), and the total fell by 40.95. It pays only where it gains: on the test set, 11 of the 16 dimensions carry between 1.195 and 2.449 nats each, with a mean σ between 0.09 and 0.32, while the other 5 stay on the prior, with at most 0.0016 nats and a mean σ between 0.98 and 1.00. A dimension costs KL as soon as it carries anything, and the optimizer leaves these 5 unused.

    Why Figure 3 looks worse than Figure 2: two reasons.

    1. Where the prior samples land. The decoder is trained only on codes drawn from q(z | x) for training images, and the KL pulls each of those Gaussians toward N(0, I) without making the cloud they form together equal to N(0, I). That mismatch is the second term of the split above, and it is far from zero. With 50 000 training images the code can carry at most log 50 000 = 10.82 nats about which image it came from, so of the mean training KL of 19.51 nats at least 8.69 nats are KL(q(z) ‖ p(z)). Measured directly: at the codes of the test images, the density of the cloud of training codes has a median log q(z) of −31.28 and a 1st percentile of −73.02. At the 64 codes of Figure 3 the median is −51.32, 20.04 nats lower, and 10 of the 64 codes fall below that 1st percentile, where 1% of 64 would be 0.64. Those samples come from regions where the decoder was hardly trained ("off the manifold, nothing was trained"), and that is where shapes that are no digit at all can come from.
    2. What the decoder outputs for a given z. The decoder does not output an image, it outputs the probability of each pixel, which is the mean of p(x | z). If several images are consistent with a code, the loss-minimizing output is their average, and averages of digits whose strokes fall in slightly different places are grey where real digits are black or white (the lecture's section on blur: maximum likelihood is mode-covering and returns the conditional mean). The share of grey pixels, strictly between 0.1 and 0.9, shows it: 8.59% in the test images, 14.53% in their reconstructions from μ and 16.53% in the samples of Figure 3, the blurriest of the three.

    Why μ in Figure 2. μ is the centre of q(z | x), the single most likely code for the image. A sampled z = μ + σε moves the code by the encoder's uncertainty, a mean σ between 0.09 and 0.32 on the active dimensions, toward codes of other images. The reconstruction would then change from one draw to the next and show that noise on top of what the code keeps. Decoding μ shows the code itself, the same image every time, and it is also the better reconstruction: 77.02 nats on the test set against 82.30 with a sampled z.

    """Exercise 1 — a VAE on MNIST.

Trains the model of the statement with d = 16 (Figure 1), reports its test terms, draws the reconstructions
and the prior samples (Figures 2 and 3), and measures the numbers the analysis of item E rests on.
"""

import math

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import torch

from common import (BATCH_SIZE, BETA, EPOCHS, LR, SEED, TEST_SEED, VAL_SEED, grey_fraction, save, show_grid,
                    tile_ticks)
from vae import VAE, evaluate, kl_term, reconstruction_term, sample, train

D = 16
PRIOR_SEED = 0   # Figure 3: the prior samples are drawn after torch.manual_seed(0)
GRID = 8         # Figure 3 is an 8 x 8 grid


@torch.no_grad()
def log_aggregate_posterior(Z, mu, logvar, chunk=32):
    """log q(z) for each row of Z, where q(z) = (1/N) sum_n N(z; mu_n, diag(sigma_n^2)) is the aggregate posterior:
    the density of the cloud formed by the codes of all N training images together."""
    n, d = mu.shape
    inv_var = torch.exp(-logvar)
    log_norm = -0.5 * (logvar.sum(dim=1) + d * math.log(2 * math.pi))     # log of each Gaussian's normalizer
    out = []
    for start in range(0, len(Z), chunk):
        z = Z[start:start + chunk]
        sq = ((z[:, None, :] - mu[None]) ** 2 * inv_var[None]).sum(dim=-1)  # (chunk, N) squared Mahalanobis
        out.append(torch.logsumexp(log_norm[None] - 0.5 * sq, dim=1) - math.log(n))
    return torch.cat(out)


@torch.no_grad()
def pixel_entropy(X):
    """Per image, sum_j H(x_j) = -sum_j [x_j log x_j + (1 - x_j) log(1 - x_j)]: the smallest value the BCE can take
    on that image (reached when x_hat = x). It is 0 for black and white pixels and positive for grey ones."""
    return reconstruction_term(X, X).item()


def run(data):
    print("\n" + "=" * 72 + "\nEXERCISE 1 — a VAE on MNIST (d = 16)\n" + "=" * 72)
    X_tr, _ = data["train"]
    X_va, _ = data["val"]
    X_te, y_te = data["test"]

    # --- A: the data -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    print(f"train {tuple(X_tr.shape)}, validation {tuple(X_va.shape)}, test {tuple(X_te.shape)}; "
          f"pixel range [{X_tr.min().item():.1f}, {X_tr.max().item():.1f}]")

    # --- B: the model ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    torch.manual_seed(SEED)
    model = VAE(D)
    n_parameters = sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters())
    print(f"VAE with d = {D}: {n_parameters} parameters")

    # --- C: training, both terms logged separately -----------------------------------------------
    history = train(model, X_tr, X_va, EPOCHS, BATCH_SIZE, LR, BETA, VAL_SEED)
    print(f"untrained model, validation: reconstruction {history['val_rec'][0]:.2f}, KL {history['val_kl'][0]:.4f}")
    print("epoch | train rec   train KL | val rec   val KL   val total")
    for e in range(EPOCHS):
        print(f"{e + 1:5d} | {history['train_rec'][e]:9.2f} {history['train_kl'][e]:9.2f} | "
              f"{history['val_rec'][e + 1]:7.2f} {history['val_kl'][e + 1]:7.2f} "
              f"{history['val_rec'][e + 1] + history['val_kl'][e + 1]:9.2f}")
    test_rec, test_kl = evaluate(model, X_te, TEST_SEED)
    print(f"TEST (d = {D}): reconstruction {test_rec:.2f}, KL {test_kl:.2f}, negative ELBO {test_rec + test_kl:.2f} "
          f"nats per image")

    # Figure 1: the two terms per epoch, training and validation
    epochs = np.arange(1, EPOCHS + 1)
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4.2))
    for ax, term, name in ((axes[0], "rec", "reconstruction (BCE)"), (axes[1], "kl", "KL to the prior")):
        ax.plot(epochs, history[f"train_{term}"], "o-", ms=3, color="tab:blue",
                label="training (average over the epoch)")

    fig.suptitle(f"Figure 1 — the two loss terms during training (d = {D}, β = {BETA:g}, Adam lr {LR:g}, "
                 f"batch {BATCH_SIZE})")
    save(fig, "fig1.png")

    model.eval()
    first = [int(torch.nonzero(y_te == k)[0]) for k in range(10)]  # first test image of each digit 0-9
    with torch.no_grad():
        mu_first, _ = model.encode(X_te[first])
        recon_first = model.decode(mu_first)
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 2.6))
    show_grid(ax, torch.cat([X_te[first], recon_first]), 2, 10)
    tile_ticks(ax, "x", [f"{k}\n#{i}" for k, i in enumerate(first)])

    """Checks of the hand-written parts of vae.py against independent computations.

1. The reconstruction term equals F.binary_cross_entropy(..., reduction="sum") divided by the batch size, the
   call the statement names, in value; its gradient, autograd's exact derivative, equals the built-in's
   wherever the built-in does not floor x_hat (1 - x_hat) at 1e-12 (and reduction="mean" is 784 times smaller).
2. The closed-form KL equals a Monte Carlo estimate of E_q[log q(z) - log p(z)], with both log-densities
   written out, within the estimate's standard error.
3. The reparameterization passes the gradients dz/dmu = 1 and dz/dlogvar = sigma * eps / 2.
"""

import math

import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

from common import VAL_SEED
from vae import N_PIXELS, reconstruction_term, reparameterize

MC_SAMPLES = 200_000
BUILTIN_FLOOR = 1e-12  # the floor F.binary_cross_entropy's backward puts on x_hat (1 - x_hat)


def log_normal(z, mu, logvar):
    """log N(z; mu, diag(exp(logvar))), summed over the last dimension."""
    return -0.5 * (math.log(2 * math.pi) + logvar + (z - mu) ** 2 / logvar.exp()).sum(dim=-1)


def run(model, X_val):
    print("\n" + "=" * 72 + "\nLOSS CHECK — the hand-written terms against independent computations\n" + "=" * 72)
    model.eval()
    generator = torch.Generator().manual_seed(VAL_SEED)

    # --- 1. reconstruction term vs F.binary_cross_entropy ----------------------------------------
    # Values: the same formula, the same -100 floor on each log. Gradients: autograd differentiates our formula
    # exactly, (x_hat - x) / (x_hat (1 - x_hat)) per pixel; the built-in's backward divides by
    # max(x_hat (1 - x_hat), 1e-12) instead, so the two can only differ where that product is below 1e-12.
    value_error = grad_error = builtin_error = 0.0
    n_pixels = n_floored = n_floored_ink = 0
    for start in range(0, len(X_val), 1000):
        x = X_val[start:start + 1000]
        with torch.no_grad():
            x_hat, _, _ = model(x, generator)
        ours_in = x_hat.clone().requires_grad_(True)
        ref_in = x_hat.clone().requires_grad_(True)
        ours = reconstruction_term(ours_in, x)
        ref = F.binary_cross_entropy(ref_in, x, reduction="sum") / len(x)
        ours.backward()
        ref.backward()
        value_error = max(value_error, abs(ours.item() - ref.item()) / ref.item())
        product = x_hat * (1 - x_hat)
        regular = product >= BUILTIN_FLOOR
        grad_error = max(grad_error, ((ours_in.grad - ref_in.grad)[regular].abs().max()
                                      / ref_in.grad[regular].abs().max()).item())
        builtin = (x_hat - x) / product.clamp(min=BUILTIN_FLOOR) / len(x)
        builtin_error = max(builtin_error, ((ref_in.grad - builtin).abs().max() / builtin.abs().max()).item())
        n_pixels += x.numel()
        n_floored += int((~regular).sum())
        n_floored_ink += int((~regular & (x > 0)).sum())
    saturated = torch.zeros_like(x)  # x and x_hat: the last chunk of the loop
    saturated[:, ::2] = 1.0  # outputs exactly 0 and 1: both versions floor each log at -100
    edge_ours = reconstruction_term(saturated, x).item()
    edge_ref = F.binary_cross_entropy(saturated, x, reduction="sum").item() / len(x)
    ratio = (F.binary_cross_entropy(x_hat, x, reduction="sum") / len(x)
             / F.binary_cross_entropy(x_hat, x, reduction="mean")).item()
    print(f"reconstruction term vs F.binary_cross_entropy(sum) / batch, on the {len(X_val)} validation images "
          f"(trained d = 16 model):")
    print(f"  value: largest relative difference {value_error:.1e}")
    print(f"  gradient: largest relative difference {grad_error:.1e} on the pixels with x_hat (1 - x_hat) >= "
          f"{BUILTIN_FLOOR:g}; the built-in's gradient is (x_hat - x) / max(x_hat (1 - x_hat), {BUILTIN_FLOOR:g}) "
          f"(relative difference {builtin_error:.1e}),")
    print(f"  so it is smaller than the exact derivative on the {n_floored} of {n_pixels} pixels "
          f"({n_floored / n_pixels:.2%}) below that floor, {n_floored_ink} of them with ink (x > 0)")
    print(f"  outputs saturated at exactly 0 and 1: {edge_ours:.4f} vs {edge_ref:.4f}; "
          f"sum / batch over reduction='mean': {ratio:.2f} (= {N_PIXELS} pixels)")

    # --- 2. closed-form KL vs Monte Carlo --------------------------------------------------------
    with torch.no_grad():
        mu, logvar = model.encode(X_val[:5])
    mc_gen = torch.Generator().manual_seed(0)
    worst_z = worst_rel = 0.0
    for m, lv in zip(mu, logvar):
        closed = (0.5 * (m ** 2 + lv.exp() - 1 - lv)).sum().item()
        z = m + torch.exp(0.5 * lv) * torch.randn(MC_SAMPLES, len(m), generator=mc_gen)
        terms = log_normal(z, m, lv) - log_normal(z, torch.zeros_like(m), torch.zeros_like(lv))
        estimate, stderr = terms.mean().item(), terms.std().item() / math.sqrt(MC_SAMPLES)
        worst_z = max(worst_z, abs(closed - estimate) / stderr)
        worst_rel = max(worst_rel, abs(closed - estimate) / closed)
        print(f"  KL closed form {closed:8.4f} | Monte Carlo ({MC_SAMPLES} samples) {estimate:8.4f} ± {stderr:.4f}")
    print(f"closed-form KL vs Monte Carlo on 5 validation images: largest gap {worst_z:.2f} standard errors "
          f"({worst_rel:.2%} of the KL)")

    # --- 3. reparameterization gradients ---------------------------------------------------------
    mu_r = mu.clone().requires_grad_(True)
    logvar_r = logvar.clone().requires_grad_(True)
    eps = torch.randn(mu.shape, generator=torch.Generator().manual_seed(1))
    z = reparameterize(mu_r, logvar_r, torch.Generator().manual_seed(1))  # the same eps
    z.sum().backward()
    reparam_error = max((mu_r.grad - 1).abs().max().item(),
                        (logvar_r.grad - 0.5 * torch.exp(0.5 * logvar) * eps).abs().max().item())
    print(f"reparameterization: largest |autograd - (1, sigma * eps / 2)| = {reparam_error:.1e}")
    return {"bce_value_rel_error": value_error, "bce_grad_rel_error_regular": grad_error,

            "n_floored_ink": n_floored_ink,
            "bce_saturated": [edge_ours, edge_ref], "sum_over_mean_ratio": ratio,
            "kl_mc_worst_stderr": worst_z, "kl_mc_worst_rel": worst_rel, "reparam_grad_error": reparam_error}

    """Majority digit among the k nearest references (Euclidean distance) of each query; ties go to the smaller

def sci(x):
    """1.234e-08 -> '1.2 × 10⁻⁸', the report's notation."""
    mantissa, exponent = f"{x:.1e}".split("e")
